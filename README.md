## Maker vs. taker fees

The maker/taker distinction is one of the most important details in any crypto exchange fee comparison.

A **taker** order executes immediately against orders already sitting in the order book. Market orders usually behave this way. A taker removes liquidity from the market and normally pays the higher fee.

A **maker** order adds liquidity by resting on the order book before it is matched. A limit order may qualify as a maker order if it does not execute immediately.

The order type alone does not decide the rate. A limit order that matches instantly can still be charged the taker rate. OKX determines the fee from the actual execution behavior of the order.

Here is the practical difference:

| Order situation | Likely classification | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Market order filled immediately | Taker | Higher fee |
| Limit order rests on the book | Maker | Lower fee |
| Limit order crosses the current book and fills immediately | Taker | Higher fee |
| Partially resting, partially immediate order | Maker and taker portions may both apply | Mixed |

For active traders, this difference can become meaningful. On a $10,000 trade, a 0.3500% taker fee equals $35. A 0.2000% maker fee equals $20. That is a $15 difference on one side of one trade, before considering the cost of closing the position.

A round trip includes both entry and exit. If both sides execute as takers, the commission is charged twice.

## How much would a typical spot trade cost?

Assume a regular account buys $1,000 of BTC through an order that executes entirely as a taker.

At a 0.3500% taker rate:

text
$1,000 × 0.0035 = $3.50


The same $1,000 trade at the 0.2000% maker rate would cost:

text
$1,000 × 0.0020 = $2.00


If you later sell the same $1,000 position, another fee applies. A simplified round-trip estimate would therefore be:

| Execution pattern | Entry fee | Exit fee | Approximate total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Maker in, maker out | $2.00 | $2.00 | $4.00 |
| Taker in, maker out | $3.50 | $2.00 | $5.50 |
| Maker in, taker out | $2.00 | $3.50 | $5.50 |
| Taker in, taker out | $3.50 | $3.50 | $7.00 |

This example assumes the same $1,000 notional on both sides and ignores price movement, spread, slippage, and any referral rebate. Real fills can differ because the order may execute at several prices or in multiple parts.

For spot trading, the fee may be deducted from the asset received or from sale proceeds, depending on the transaction. That is why the amount displayed in an order confirmation can differ slightly from the final amount credited to the account.

## Futures fees are based on position size

Futures traders need to be more careful because the fee is calculated against the notional value of the position.

Using OKX’s own type of calculation, a $20,000 USDT-margined futures position at a 0.0500% taker fee would cost:

text
$20,000 × 0.0005 = $10


If the position is opened and closed at the same notional value, the trading fees would be approximately $20 before funding, slippage, and any other applicable costs.

The $2,000 margin in a 10x example does not reduce the fee base to $2,000. The exchange applies the fee to the $20,000 position value. This is one of the easiest ways for new derivatives traders to underestimate their cost.

Futures can also involve:

- **Funding fees** for perpetual contracts
- **Liquidation fees** if a position is forcibly closed
- **Expiry settlement fees** for contracts that expire
- Spread and execution costs caused by market conditions

Funding is separate from the trading commission. It is exchanged between long and short traders, and the direction can change. When the funding rate is positive, longs generally pay shorts; when it is negative, shorts generally pay longs. The settlement frequency depends on the specific contract and can vary.

A low futures commission therefore does not automatically mean a low total cost. A position held through several funding settlements may cost more than the entry and exit commission suggests.

- A screen showing “zero trading fee” does not necessarily mean the transaction has no cost.
- A separate commission line may be absent because the cost is already reflected in the quoted price.

For a small, occasional conversion, convenience may matter more than the exact fee format. For larger or frequent trades, compare the final amount received with the order-book alternatives instead of looking only for a “0% fee” label.

## What other costs should traders include?

A proper crypto exchange fee comparison should include more than the headline commission.

### Trading spread

The spread is the difference between available buy and sell prices. It becomes more important during volatile markets, for less liquid assets, and when using instant conversion tools.

### Slippage

Slippage occurs when the final execution price differs from the price visible when the order was submitted. Large market orders and thinly traded pairs are more exposed.

### Funding

Perpetual futures may incur funding payments at scheduled intervals. Funding is not fixed in the same way as a trading commission and can move with market conditions.

### Liquidation

A forced liquidation can involve a fee charged at the account’s taker rate. It also exposes the trader to the consequences of rapid price movement and reduced margin.

### Withdrawal fees

Crypto withdrawal costs depend on the asset and network. They are separate from the trading fee and can change when network conditions or platform rules change. Always check the amount displayed on the withdrawal screen before confirming.

### Fiat payment costs

Buying crypto with a bank card or other payment method can involve a third-party processing charge, payment spread, or local banking fee. These costs may not appear as the same line item as the exchange trading commission.

## How to reduce your real trading cost

The fee schedule is only useful if you apply it to the way you trade. These steps usually matter more than chasing a small headline difference between exchanges.

### Use limit orders when execution allows it

A limit order can receive maker pricing if it rests on the order book. However, do not assume every limit order qualifies. If it fills immediately, the taker rate can apply.

For urgent entries or exits, paying the taker fee may be reasonable because execution certainty matters. Saving a fraction of a percent is not useful if the order remains unfilled while the market moves.

### Check the fee before confirming the trade

OKX displays the applicable fee rate in the order placement panel. The exact final amount may change if the order fills in parts or at different prices, but the panel gives you the correct starting point for estimating the commission.

### Calculate the round trip

A trader who only calculates the entry fee is looking at half the cost. Estimate both the opening and closing transaction.

For futures, include funding if the position may remain open across a settlement period.

### Separate spot and futures volume

OKX calculates fee tiers by product line in several cases. Spot volume and derivatives volume are not automatically interchangeable for every fee calculation. The platform states that users should check the relevant fee rules for their account and instrument.

A lower fee rate does not justify unnecessary volume. Generating $1 million in trading volume to save a few basis points can be a poor trade if the strategy itself is not profitable after spread, slippage, and market risk.
